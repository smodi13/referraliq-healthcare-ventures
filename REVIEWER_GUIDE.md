# REVIEWER_GUIDE

## What this is
ReferralIQ is an independent portfolio project by a candidate for the Investment Analyst, Ventures role. It is not prepared, commissioned or endorsed by Intermountain Health or Intermountain Ventures, and it is not connected to any of their systems. It uses public information and clearly labeled hypothetical scenarios, and it is not a live data feed.

## The question
Should a health-system venture group explore an investment in Silna Health, a commercial pilot, alternative referral and prior authorization AI vendors, or defer until key evidence exists? The commercial decision (does a controlled pilot make sense) and the equity decision (would an investment make sense at verifiable terms) are kept separate.

## Current recommendation
Advance to structured diligence. Equity investment is not supported on public information. A pilot is conditional on confirming the incumbent gap (Notable) and a safety and security review. Tennr is the product benchmark; Rhyme is the payer-provider workflow comparator.

## Method
1. Twelve companies were screened on six dimensions rated 1 to 5 with analyst weights of 25, 20, 15, 15, 15 and 10 (score = weighted sum divided by 5). These are judgment scores, not predictions.
2. Each company claim links to a dated source register entry (36 sources) with its limitations. Vendor-authored sources are labeled as vendor claims.
3. A three-scenario operating model (90-day pilot, annual run-rate, first scaled year) and a venture returns model use a frozen contract; outputs were reconciled to the Excel workbook and a second implementation.
4. Ten diligence gates define what must be true before any commitment. All start Unknown.

## 90-second walkthrough
1. Overview (15 s): read the recommendation card and the 90-second brief; note the six evidence labels (reported, verified, vendor-reported, analyst judgment, modeled, unverified) and the screening limitation on Silna's entry rating.
2. Deal Screener (15 s): open the table, see why Tennr scores highest while Silna has the better entry rating; open the weight sandbox.
3. Pilot Economics (30 s): move cash realization from 50% to 30% and watch run-rate net cash turn negative; note break-even at 41.3% and that the pilot loses money by design.
4. Venture Returns (20 s): read gross MOIC 2.97x in the Base case, then the heatmap and the 3.0x reverse-underwriting figure (about $80.8m exit ARR, labeled illustrative).
5. Evidence and Gates (10 s): ten gates, all Unknown, with G01, G07 and G08 as the blockers.

## What to inspect first
The 90-second brief on the Overview, then the Base scenario waterfall on Pilot Economics, then the gate table.

## Which numbers are hypothetical
All pilot costs and savings, cash realization, entry valuation, round size, check size, exit ARR, multiples and dilution. Historical financing headlines (for example Silna $27M, March 2025) are public statements, not current terms.

## What it demonstrates
Deal sourcing and screening, competitive research, financial modeling and reconciliation, due diligence structure, investment judgment under uncertainty, Python implementation with tests, and clear communication of what is and is not known.

## What it does not claim
No guaranteed insight, no AI prediction of company success, no verified private company metrics, no statement that Intermountain uses or will use any product, no net fund IRR.
